## 1. Verdict table

| Question | MiniMax H3 | Wan 2.2 |
|---|---|---|
| Train another model on its outputs (distillation)? | **NO** — §V.3 prohibits | YES — Apache-2.0 has no such restriction |

## 2. Exact clauses — MiniMax H3 Community License

### 2.1 Distillation ban (dispositive)

§I.11 ("Model Derivatives") defines what counts as a derivative:

> "**Model Derivatives** means all of the following: (i) any modification of
> MiniMax H3 or any Model Derivative thereof; (ii) any work based on MiniMax H3
> or any Model Derivative thereof; or (iii) any other machine learning model
> created by transferring the patterns of the weights, parameters, operational
> patterns, or Outputs of MiniMax H3 or any Model Derivative thereof to another
> model, such that the latter model exhibits behavior similar to MiniMax H3 or
> its Model Derivatives, **including by distillation methods**, methods using
> intermediate data representations, **or methods based on training using
> synthetic-data Outputs generated by MiniMax H3 or its Model Derivatives.**
> For the avoidance of doubt, Outputs are not deemed Model Derivatives."

§V.3 (Use Restrictions) then bans exactly this use for *other* models:

> "You may not use the MiniMax H3 Works or any of their Outputs or results to
> improve any other artificial intelligence model (other than MiniMax H3 or its
> Model Derivatives)."

Reading: creating Model Derivatives *of H3* (e.g. fine-tuning H3) is granted in
§II; but training our student bridge — a different architecture trained on
teacher frames — is "training using synthetic-data Outputs" / "distillation"
and hits §V.3 head-on. The final sentence of §I.11 ("Outputs are not deemed
Model Derivatives") does not rescue this: it means possessing outputs doesn't
make you a derivative-holding licensee, while §V.3 independently forbids using
those outputs to improve any non-H3 model.

**→ Student v0 training on H3 teacher frames: NO-GO.**

### 2.2 Territorial ban (independently dispositive)

§I.5 and §I.3:

> "'Excluded Territories' means the European Union, the United Kingdom, the
> Republic of Korea **and the United States of America**."
>
> "'Applicable Territory' means worldwide, excluding the Excluded Territories."

§II:

> "Solely within the Applicable Territory, we grant you a non-exclusive,
> non-transferable, royalty-free, limited license to use, reproduce, …"

§V.4:

> "You may not use, reproduce, modify, distribute, or display the MiniMax H3
> Works or any of their Outputs or results outside the Applicable Territory.
> Any such use outside the Applicable Territory is not authorized by this
> Agreement."

Our training and serving infrastructure (simforge1, 216.151.21.122, US-hosted;
local workstation in the US) sits inside an Excluded Territory. Even the W0
translation runs already performed were, strictly read, uses "outside the
Applicable Territory". This is a second, independent NO-GO that no amount of
prompting or conditioning work fixes.

### 2.3 Commercial terms (informational)

§IV.1: separate written authorization required if commercial products/services
exceed US$20M yearly revenue. Not our binding constraint today, but relevant to
any future commercialization path built on H3.

## 3. Wan 2.2 — verified Apache-2.0

All three Wan 2.2 repositories carry card metadata `license: apache-2.0` and
the README statement:

> "The models in this repository are licensed under the Apache 2.0 License. We
> claim no rights over the your generated contents, granting you the freedom to
> use them while ensuring that your usage complies with the provisions of this
> license."

Apache-2.0 grants the right to create and distribute derivative works (§2) and
imposes **no restriction on training other models on the model's outputs**.
Training our few-step conditional student on Wan 2.2 translations of our
renders is unambiguously permitted, including commercially, with attribution
and the standard NOTICE requirements.

Note (housekeeping): the Wan READMEs reference a full-text `LICENSE.txt` that
is not present in the repo trees at time of writing; the card-level
`license: apache-2.0` tag plus the quoted README paragraph is the evidence of
record. If Wan 2.2 becomes load-bearing for a shipped product, snapshot the
Apache-2.0 text into `THIRD_PARTY_ASSETS.md` at integration time.

## 4. What happens to the existing H3 artifacts and pipeline

1. **Existing H3 translated clips** (`simforge1:~/w0-data/translated{,-pov}/`,
   `~/w0-exp/e*/`): retained strictly as *evaluation references* for the W0/V2
   audits already reported (W0_REPORT.md, V2_RESULTS.md). They are NOT used as
   training targets, style targets, or distillation signal anywhere in
   `tools/bridge-student/`.
2. **H3 audit tooling findings stand**: the V2 isolation matrix (E1–E6) remains
   valid evidence about Ref2VA edit-mode semantics binding; nothing in the
   license decision changes the technical conclusions.
3. **Student v0 training pairs**: the (render frame, G-buffer conditioning)
   half of every pair comes from our own engine/renderer; the RGB target for
   training is produced by **Wan 2.2 I2V** translation runs (generation command
   path documented in `tools/bridge-student/README.md` and the scale-out memo).
   No H3 output enters the training corpus.
4. **If MiniMax is ever contacted** (e.g. for an Excluded-Territory license per
   §II: "should any person in such Excluded Territories be interested in
   deploying our models, you are welcome to contact us"), H3 could be
   re-evaluated as teacher under negotiated terms; until then H3 stays demoted
   to eval-reference-only per plan WS3 item 4.

## 5. Consequence for the plan

Per hardening-plan WS3 item 4: "If restricted, Wan 2.2 (Apache-2.0) becomes
primary teacher; H3 demoted to eval reference." **Both trigger conditions fired**
(§V.3 restriction AND Excluded-Territory status). Decision executed: all
student-training generation moves to Wan 2.2 I2V; the frozen-perception
auditor gates every teacher's outputs identically regardless of source.
